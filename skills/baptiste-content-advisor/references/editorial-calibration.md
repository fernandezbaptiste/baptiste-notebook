# Editorial calibration

## Confirmed preferences

From Baptiste's conversation through 12 November 2026:

- Content should help him and his audience learn about DevRel, developer growth and AI; each post should add value or entertain.
- Concrete examples from other startups are welcome. Invented personal or Tessl experiences, roadmaps and claims are not.
- He wants authority balanced with humility; suggestive interpretations suit him better than prescriptions.
- Credit Reforge where its frameworks are used. Do not manufacture a connection to Tessl in every post.
- He sets an 8.75/10 reader-value bar. Generic advice and vague implications have repeatedly missed it.

## Positive anchor: PostHog's comparison content

Source: Natalia Amorim, [How to write comparison content that doesn't age like milk](https://posthog.com/blog/comparison-content-guide), 27 October 2026. Source read again on 12 November 2026.

Source detail: PostHog accepts competitor corrections, including an Amplitude contribution correcting four feature claims. The company describes publishing fair comparisons and maintaining shared competitor data.

Baptiste's explicit feedback, 12 November: "something that I would want to read"; "with everything you've shown me here ... that's actually the best thing."

His preferred framing: a team can reasonably opt out of competitor comparisons, while still finding it interesting to understand the stance of PostHog, whose DevRel and content work he respects. He preferred this over centering "we" or declaring a Tessl position.

What this supports: useful editorial value can come from examining a credible team's choice without endorsing or copying it. The credibility assessment is Baptiste's view, not an independently established ranking of DevRel teams.

Not yet established: whether he prefers strategic choices over technical experiments generally; how much technical detail he enjoys; tolerance for older sources; whether he wants humour in every post. Do not infer rejection of the other initial articles from his choice of PostHog.

## Copy correction received on 12 November 2026

Baptiste rejected all nine first drafts, while keeping the three selected topics: Resend customer stories, Supabase launch process, and PostHog comparisons. The issue was the writing, not a rejection of those subjects.

- Keep attention on the content and its value to operators, marketers and DevRel practitioners. He specifically rejected "caught my attention", "stuck with me", "made me think" and similar first-person reactions.
- Write story-like, connected paragraphs. The sequence of clipped sentences and the fabricated joke about live debugging and sleep sounded AI-written to him.
- Be suggestive and describe tradeoffs; there is no single right answer for every product team. A company example should invite consideration, not prescribe a tactic.
- Resend: use the annotated Developer Markepear image he added. Describe its fundamentals: quote as evidence, identifiable customer, succinct problem, highlighted benefits, and the reason a reader should care. Credit Developer Markepear and Resend separately.
- Supabase: start with Ant Wilson's story, develop the product-team lesson, then introduce Tessl's polish week as supporting experience. Do not turn the whole piece into a Tessl promotion or imply the source caused Tessl's decision.
- PostHog: start from the decision about whether developer products should publish comparisons. Include article details such as reported signups, reader intent, meaningful feature or audience differences, fair treatment of competitors, and maintenance where useful. Allow for a reasonable choice to focus on one's own product instead.
- User supplied a corrected figure of more than 20,000 developers reading the Tessl blog monthly, and said the team is working on clear problems, benefits and reasons to care. Treat this as his supplied context, not an independently audited metric. The spoken phrase about "hundreds of dollars" was ambiguous and should not become a claim.

The revised copies still need Baptiste's judgment; do not mark them approved or claim they clear 8.75/10 based on an internal score.

## Second copy correction received on 12 November 2026

Baptiste found the revisions better, but still too long, too authoritative and visually heavy. His bar is that every word adds value; additional explanation is welcome only when it adds something new.

- Keep the Resend reader-problem angle: understanding what a tool does may not establish a reason to use or buy it. Frame this as a possibility, not a universal diagnosis.
- Prioritise tangible observations from the annotated example: the headline names the developers it serves, a named customer's quote supplies evidence, and specific benefits explain the practical value. He called the audience-specific wording a CTA; the image itself places it in the headline, so preserve the preference without mislabelling the element.
- Keep copy short and suggestive. Do not replace useful detail with a generic lesson or a statement of authority.
- Vary spacing and structure. Neither long consecutive paragraphs nor one isolated sentence after another matches the requested reading experience.
- Allow occasional lowercase and omitted full stops for a casually written feel. This is permission for variation, not a requirement to make every post lowercase or deliberately incorrect.
- Explicitly avoid "the difficult part is" and comparable filler announcing the point instead of making it.
