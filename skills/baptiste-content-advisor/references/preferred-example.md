# Preferred direction

Baptiste preferred the direct, contextual Resend version below to the later newsletter experiment. This is a writing reference, not proof of approval to publish.

If you’re writing about a developer product, this Resend customer story offers a few ideas for answering the reader’s “why should I care?” early on.

It describes Layer’s experience with Resend’s email API, all on one screen:

• Name the audience. The headline points to developers across the team at Layer, making it easy to recognise who the story is for.

• Give the reader evidence. Layer’s co-founder says he had Resend set up in their codebase within 30 seconds. The quote tells you something specific about getting started.

• Explain what became easier. Any developer on the team can create an email template and start using it in the product.

We’re working on this across the Tessl blog too, which reaches more than 20,000 developers a month. Making the problem and the expected benefit clear upfront gives someone a reason to spend their time with an article.

Perhaps a useful check before publishing: can a developer quickly tell whether it’s for them, what they’ll learn, and how that might help their work? 📝
